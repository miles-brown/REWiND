# REWiND Fact & Claim Standard

## 1. Claim-Level Decomposition

In the REWiND Evidence Atlas, an event is not treated as a monolithic indivisible truth. Rather, an event is a collection of discrete, individually supportable **factual claims**.

*Example: 4 February 2025 White House Meeting*
- **Claim 1**: Benjamin Netanyahu was physically present in the White House.
- **Claim 2**: Donald Trump was physically present in the White House.
- **Claim 3**: The bilateral meeting took place in the Oval Office.
- **Claim 4**: The meeting commenced at approximately 16:12 local civil time.
- **Claim 5**: The participants discussed humanitarian aid and security agreements.
- **Claim 6**: Benjamin Netanyahu delivered a statement to reporters upon departure.
- **Claim 7**: Total meeting duration was approximately 54 minutes.

Each claim possesses its own epistemic classification, evidentiary attachments, and verification status.

---

## 2. Controlled Claim Statuses

REWiND uses controlled editorial classifications for factual claims:

1. **`ESTABLISHED`**: Strong, direct, and/or independently corroborated primary evidence leaves no reasonable doubt regarding the proposition.
2. **`STRONGLY SUPPORTED`**: Persuasive evidence with no meaningful contradictions, but reliant on indirect, inferential, or incomplete documentation.
3. **`SUPPORTED`**: Credible evidence reasonably supports the proposition, but further independent primary corroboration is required.
4. **`PROVISIONAL`**: Credible contemporaneous reports exist, but primary records or transcripts have not yet been indexed.
5. **`UNVERIFIED`**: An assertion exists in the historical record, but adequate supporting evidence has not been obtained.
6. **`DISPUTED`**: Multiple credible sources materially disagree on essential factual elements.
7. **`CONTRADICTED`**: High-tier evidence weighs directly against the assertion.
8. **`DEMONSTRABLY FALSE`**: Unambiguous evidence establishes that the proposition did not occur or is fabricated.
9. **`UNKNOWN`**: Insufficient evidentiary basis exists to evaluate the proposition.

---

## 3. Epistemic Classification: Fact, Assertion, Opinion

Every substantive statement in REWiND is classified into one of the following epistemic classes:

- `observed fact`: Directly recorded by continuous audiovisual devices or neutral institutional sensors.
- `documented fact`: Established by contemporaneous written records of unimpeached provenance.
- `derived/computed fact`: Calculated deterministically (e.g., day of week from calendar date, transit velocity from distance/time).
- `attributed assertion`: A documented statement made by a participant or speaker.
- `expert interpretation`: Analysis provided by recognized professional, academic, or forensic authorities.
- `editorial inference`: Deductive synthesis reached by REWiND editors, transparently flagged as inferential.
- `opinion`: Subjective viewpoints, evaluations, or rhetorical arguments.
- `allegation`: Formal or informal claims of wrongdoing made prior to judicial determination.
- `disputed proposition`: Active historical controversies with competing evidentiary records.
- `unknown`: Epistemic status not yet determined.

---

## 4. Attributed Claims vs. Established Events

Where evidence proves only that a figure made a statement, REWiND does **not** promote the substance of that statement into an established historical fact:

- *Statement*: Politician A states: *"I met with President B in Geneva yesterday."*
- *Claim 1 (Attributed Assertion)*: *"Politician A publicly claimed to have met President B in Geneva."* $\rightarrow$ `ESTABLISHED` (proven by video of press conference).
- *Claim 2 (Physical Event)*: *"Politician A met President B in Geneva."* $\rightarrow$ `UNVERIFIED` or `PROVISIONAL` until independent confirmation from Swiss protocol or President B's records is obtained.

---

## 5. Judicial & Legal Precision

Legal statuses must be recorded with exact terminology. Broad colloquialisms that obscure legal realities are strictly prohibited:

$$\text{alleged} \longrightarrow \text{investigated} \longrightarrow \text{charged} \longrightarrow \text{indicted} \longrightarrow \text{tried} \longrightarrow \text{convicted} \mid \text{acquitted}$$

Additional exact statuses:
- `civilly liable`
- `appeal pending`
- `conviction overturned`
- `case dismissed with prejudice`
- `case dismissed without prejudice`
- `settled without admission of liability`
